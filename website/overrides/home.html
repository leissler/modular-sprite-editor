A sprite editor for retro console games

Draw the parts once.
Build every pose

Cut your sprite sheet into body parts, assemble poses, animate them step by step and stage whole scenes.

Classic console games saved memory by building characters from small shared pieces. Modular Sprite Editor lets you work the same way, and shows how your sprites fit the Mega Drive while you draw.

From a sheet of parts to a scene

Four steps, one project.

Each step builds on the one before. Change a part and every pose, animation and scene that uses it changes with it.

01

Cut the sheet into modules

Drag a box around each part, or let the editor find the sprites on the sheet.
02

Build poses from parts

Place, flip and turn parts to make a frame. Reuse the same pieces in every pose.
03

Animate step by step

Set how long each pose holds, watch it play like the game will, compare with onion skin.
04

Stage a scene

Put characters on a timeline, move them with keys and switch their animations on cue.
Try it with your own sheet

Variants without redrawing

One set of animations.
As many characters as you like.

A mapping swaps some parts for others in every frame at once. Recolour a costume, change the armour, hide a weapon, or turn the hero into an enemy.

In the example project, the hooded guard is the Prince with 28 head and body parts swapped. He uses all of the Prince's animations without a single extra frame.

Know what the console can show

See the limits
before your programmer does.

The Mega Drive draws characters from hardware sprites, with a limit per screen and per line. Turn on the budget overlay and the editor outlines every hardware sprite a pose needs and marks the lines where sprites would vanish.
